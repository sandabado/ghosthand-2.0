## The Fork and return loop

The Fork has exactly two choices: 主 → Dojo and 縛 → Chains Hold. Both halves pivot around the shared graphic blade-line. A damped analytic spring carries position and velocity from the shared heartbeat into the DOM and GPU. Pointer proximity and keyboard focus aim the hinge; they do not commit a route.

Each half is a readable article with a dedicated grip button at least 52px high. Commitment uses press → net travel of at least 40 finite pixels → release. A click, short stroke, or excursion that returns below the threshold does not commit. Primary-pointer capture associates move/release with the originating pointer; cancellation, lost capture, blur, Escape, and motion-preference changes clear uncommitted input. Secondary pointers do not become a second commitment.

Keyboard commitment is Space to arm, ArrowDown to draw, and Enter to commit. ArrowUp resets the drawn keyboard stroke; Escape cancels. There is no plain-click shortcut. The first accepted choice is guarded, disables both grips, and starts the rejected half's collapse into an authored particle field. After that local response settles, the route changes once. Reduced motion keeps the same deliberate choice, then uses the static destination directly.

Chains Hold remains a quiet room, not a sales route. There is one green doorway returning to Fork. Scrolling through it clears the old Fork choice and prepares a fresh choice surface; the journey's Pursuit-used flag and sword/shing accounting remain intact. Persistent navigation continues to work outside the physical doorway. The holding route does not go directly to Oath.
